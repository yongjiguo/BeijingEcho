## A 级补充（11 篇）

### 26. beijing-zoo — Beijing Zoo — landmark: "the Panda House"
You've reached the Beijing Zoo — China's oldest zoo, opening in nineteen zero six as an imperial experiment garden, and home to its most beloved ambassadors: the giant pandas. The Panda House is the heart of any visit — watch the bears do what they do best: eating bamboo, up to twelve hours a day, or simply tumbling around like living plush toys. Come in the morning, when they're most active and the crowds are thinnest. But the zoo is more than pandas: over ninety years as a public zoo ⚠ 开放年份表述请核实（1908年正式开放为常见说法）, it grew into one of the world's largest, with golden monkeys, red pandas, and a lakeside setting that makes it as much park as menagerie. Families could spend half a day here; panda-only pilgrims can be done in two hours. One rule applies everywhere: quiet voices, patient eyes — the stars perform on their own schedule.

### 27. national-museum — National Museum of China — landmark: "the National Museum's colonnade on the square's east side"
On the east side of Tiananmen Square stands the National Museum of China — one of the largest museum buildings on the planet, and by visitor numbers, among the most visited on Earth. It was born in nineteen fifty-nine as one of the "Ten Great Buildings" raised for the tenth anniversary of the People's Republic. Inside: over a million and a half objects spanning five thousand years. Seek out the Houmuwu ding — the heaviest surviving bronze vessel of the ancient world, cast three thousand years ago for a royal mother. ⚠ 后母戊鼎重832.84公斤表述请核实。From oracle bones to Tang dynastic paintings to the exhibits tracing modern China's own story, this museum narrates the civilization end to end. Entry is free, but reserve ahead and bring your passport. Give it half a day — even a rushed highlight tour takes two hours — and start early, before the school groups arrive.

### 28. capital-museum — Capital Museum — landmark: "the Capital Museum's bronze-ribbed main hall"
If the National Museum tells China's story, the Capital Museum tells Beijing's — and often, more comfortably. Founded in nineteen eighty-one and reborn in a striking new building in two thousand six — a hall of stone and a bronze-walled drum that nods to ancient Chinese forms — it gathers the city's own treasures: Shang and Zhou bronzes, imperial porcelain, folk crafts, and superb galleries walking you through how a little frontier town called Ji became Dadu, then Peking, then Beijing. ⚠ 新馆2006年开放表述请核实。The galleries are airy, the lighting kind, and the crowds a fraction of the big names — if the National Museum's queue defeated you, this is the dignified alternative. Look for the old Beijing street-life reconstruction downstairs: shopfronts, cricket cages, and courtyard sounds — old Peking rebuilt in a hallway. An hour and a half covers the highlights; the coin and porcelain rooms reward the slow.

### 29. ming-city-wall-park — Ming City Wall Relics Park — landmark: "the southeast Corner Tower"
Beside the railway station stands something remarkable: the last living piece of Beijing's medieval city wall. For five centuries, a wall of rammed earth and brick, with gates and towers, enclosed the Ming capital. When the city modernized, nearly all of it came down — but this section survived, and the park before you preserves it as a quiet, climbable ruin. At its east end rises the Corner Tower — a vast two-roofed watchtower from the fourteen hundreds, one of the largest surviving watchtowers of any Chinese city wall — its arrow slits still staring down the tracks where the trains now run. ⚠ 东南角楼建造年代（明正统年间1436-1445）表述请核实。Inside the tower, a small museum shows old Beijing's gates and defenses. In early spring, the plum garden below blooms into the city's favorite flower scene. Walk the wall's grassy base at dusk — Beijing, five hundred years ago and five hundred meters from the subway.

### 30. baita-temple — White Stupa at Miaoying Temple — landmark: "the great White Dagoba"
Turn the corner of a hutong and meet a mountain of white: the Great White Stupa of Miaoying Temple, the tallest structure in old Beijing for centuries. It was commissioned by Kublai Khan in twelve seventy-one and designed by Araniko, a young Nepalese artist whose work bridged the Himalayas — this Tibetan-style dagoba announced the new Yuan dynasty's capital to the world. ⚠ 阿尼哥设计与1271年始建为通行说法，请核实。Fifty meters of white plaster over a temple you barely see — the stupa dwarfs everything, and the best views come from the hutong alleys around it, where café terraces are built precisely to stare at it. The temple museum inside tells its story; locals pray in its courtyard as they have for seven hundred and fifty years. After renovation it reopened brighter than ever. ⚠ 近年修缮重开状态请核实。This neighborhood — Baitasi — is now one of Beijing's calmest design-and-coffee corners. Perfect half hour between the Financial Street towers and the hutongs.

### 31. dongjiaominxiang — Dongjiaominxiang Legation Quarter — landmark: "the former French post office building"
This quiet street of European facades was once the most politically charged address in Asia. Dongjiaominxiang — its name began as "Rice Lane" — became the legation quarter where foreign powers set up embassies after the eighteen sixty treaties, then fortified itself into a state-within-a-state after the Boxer Uprising of nineteen hundred: banks, post offices, hospitals, and garrisons behind their own walls, foreign territory threaded through China's capital. The buildings you pass — former bank halls, a French post office, Gothic and classical facades ⚠ 具体建筑功能与编号请核实 — stayed when the era ended, and today the lane is a peaceful, plane-tree-shaded walk through that strange chapter, minutes from Tiananmen and Qianmen. Photographers love the silence; history readers love the walls. Walk it end to end, then slip south into the hutongs. It's a fifteen-minute stroll — and one of the most surprising streets in the city.

### 32. simatai — Simatai Great Wall & Gubei Water Town — landmark: "the Fairy Maiden Tower on the Simatai ridge"
For the Great Wall's wilder soul, go to Simatai — the section where the wall stops being manicured and starts being myth. Built in the Ming dynasty and left largely as time made it, Simatai climbs steep ridges with crumbling bricks, sheer stairs, and watchtowers with names like the Fairy Maiden Tower perched on stone spires. It's the only section of the Great Wall allowed to open at night — lantern-lit towers glow above a valley of water. At its foot lies Gubei Water Town, a resort rebuilt in northern-village style: canals, stone lanes, hot-spring hotels, and the wall floating overhead in evening light. ⚠ 夜游开放与水镇业态随季节调整，行前请核实。Come late afternoon: walk the wall as the light turns gold, descend into the water town for dinner, and watch the wall light up above you. It's two hours from the city — the farthest of your wall options, and the most unforgettable.

### 33. juyongguan — Juyongguan Pass — landmark: "the Cloud Platform with marble carvings"
Juyongguan is the Great Wall's front door to Beijing — the closest great mountain pass to the city, guarding the gorge through which every invader and every emperor once traveled. The pass today shows a grand Ming-dynasty fortress rebuilt in gleaming stone: twin gatehouses, looping walls hugging the valley, and the inscription that every visitor photographs — "The First Magnificent Pass Under Heaven." The true treasure is older: inside the fortress stands the Cloud Platform, a white-marble archway from the Yuan dynasty, thirteen forty-five. ⚠ 云台建成1345年表述请核实。Walk through its tunnel and look up — the walls and ceiling are covered in thousands of carved Buddhas and inscriptions of sacred texts in six scripts: Chinese, Sanskrit, Tibetan, and more — a fourteenth-century monument to one empire's many languages. It's steeper than Badaling, quieter than Badaling, and under ninety minutes from the city. ⚠ 距离与开放状态请核实。For a wall trip without the crowds, this is the insider's choice.

### 34. soong-ching-ling — Soong Ching-ling's Former Residence — landmark: "the lakeside main hall of the former Prince Chun's garden"
Tucked on the north shore of Shichahai lies one of Beijing's most graceful hidden gardens — the former garden of Prince Chun's mansion, and for the last decades of her life, the home of Soong Ching-ling. To understand her, hold this in mind: she married Sun Yat-sen, father of modern China, and after his death became a founding figure of the People's Republic — honored as its Honorary President until her death in nineteen eighty-one. ⚠ 荣誉主席称号1981年授予表述请核实。She lived here from nineteen sixty-three: a lakeside hall where she worked, entertained, and raised doves — the dovecote still stands. The rooms keep her furniture, her books, her simple desk; the garden keeps the old aristocratic bones — rockeries, ancient trees, water. Few foreign visitors find this place, which is exactly its charm. Pair it with the Drum and Bell Towers nearby, and let the quiet of the garden explain the person better than any plaque.

### 35. silk-street — Silk Street Market — landmark: "the Silk Street market's main entrance atrium"
Silk Street is Beijing's most famous bargaining arena. It began in the nineteen eighties as an outdoor market of silk sellers along this street — foreigners discovered it, word spread, and the stall vendors eventually moved into a bright eight-floor mall you see today, keeping the old name and the old game. ⚠ 1980年代露天市场起源与迁入大楼年份（2005年迁入秀水大厦为常见说法）请核实。The rule of the house: the first price is an opening move, not a number. Start at a third of it, smile, walk away slowly, and watch the price fall with every step. Silk scarves and tailor-made shirts are the classics; tailors here can measure you and deliver a fitted coat in days. The upper floors drift into electronics and souvenirs — quality varies wildly, so buy what you can judge. No haggling skill needed to enter; it's the best free entertainment in Beijing anyway. Set a budget before you go in. Everyone says that. Everyone ignores it.

### 36. hongqiao-market — Hongqiao Pearl Market — landmark: "the Hongqiao Market building by the Temple of Heaven's east gate"
Across from the Temple of Heaven's east gate stands an eight-floor building with a glittering specialty: Hongqiao Market — known worldwide as Beijing's Pearl Market, and called by China's own gem trade "the First House of Pearls in the Capital." ⚠ "京城珍珠第一家"称号（2005年首届北京国际珍珠文化节）表述请核实。Floors three to five are wall-to-wall pearls — freshwater strands from a few dollars, saltwater specimens in velvet cases, plus gold, jade, and silk on the other floors. The truth-in-shopping rules of Beijing markets apply doubly for pearls: trust your eyes, ask for certificates on the big purchases, and know that bargaining is expected from the very first quote. Even if you buy nothing, come at mid-morning when the traders are cheerful and the aisles perform their orchestra of offers. Then walk straight into the Temple of Heaven opposite — prayer first, pearls after — the most Beijing pairing of the sacred and the retail.

## B 级主题（23 篇）

### 37. ditan-park — Temple of Earth (Ditan Park) — landmark: "the square altar terrace of the Temple of Earth"
Where heaven got a circle, earth got a square. Ditan, the Temple of Earth, was built in the fifteen thirties as the counterpart to the Temple of Heaven — and for nearly five centuries, emperors came here each summer solstice to sacrifice to the Earth deity from this stark square stone terrace, the largest of its kind. ⚠ 建于嘉靖九年1530/1531与夏至祭地表述请核实。Today it's a beloved park of old cypresses, and the city's most famous temple fair ground: every Spring Festival, Ditan hosts Beijing's biggest fair — lion dances, folk crafts, snack stalls in a happy crush of locals. The literary-minded know it from a beloved essay, "I and Ditan" by the writer Shi Tiesheng, who spent years in these alleys thinking about fate after illness — the park remembers him fondly. ⚠ 史铁生《我与地坛》提及为文化背景，可留可删。Quiet on ordinary days, roaring at New Year — Ditan is heaven's mirror and Beijing's living room.

### 38. ritan-park — Ritan Park (Temple of the Sun) — landmark: "the sun altar terrace under the cypresses"
Ritan, the Temple of the Sun, is the smallest and calmest of Beijing's imperial altars — built in the fifteen thirties, when the Ming court institutionalized worship of sun, moon, earth, and heaven across the city. Each spring equinox, the emperor traveled here to greet the rising sun from the white stone altar that still sits under the cypresses today. ⚠ 建于1530与春分祭日表述请核实。Its imperial job long gone, Ritan became one of Beijing's favorite neighborhood parks: a rock garden, a lotus pond, pavilions gifted by sister cities, and at dawn, its legendary community — retirees practicing tai chi, sword dancing, and ballroom steps with total commitment. Business travelers from the nearby embassy district jog here at lunch. If you want to see daily Beijing life at its most graceful, come at seven in the morning: the altar of the sun is now, quite literally, a stage for the people.

### 39. yuetan-park — Yuetan Park (Temple of the Moon) — landmark: "the moon altar platform"
Yuetan is the moon's address in Beijing — the Temple of the Moon, built in the fifteen thirties in the same great program of imperial altars that gave the city its Temple of Heaven and Earth. Each autumn equinox, the emperor came here at dusk to sacrifice to the moon as the harvest moon rose. ⚠ 建于1530与秋分祭月表述请核实。The ritual is gone; the atmosphere remains — and it fits the theme. This is the quietest of Beijing's altar-parks: a deep, green, low-key place of old trees and wide paths, where the city noise fades early and evening light does its best work. Locals come to play chess, practice instruments, and walk off dinner. Astronomy-minded visitors like the symmetry: heaven to the south, earth to the north, sun east, moon west — the altars once mapped the cosmos onto Beijing's map. Stand here at dusk and the design still makes sense.

### 40. taimiao — Imperial Ancestral Temple (Taimiao) — landmark: "the great hall's triple roofline among ancient cypresses"
Immediately right of the Forbidden City's gate — behind the red wall most visitors walk straight past — hides the Imperial Ancestral Temple, Taimiao: where Ming and Qing emperors worshipped their ancestors. Built in fourteen twenty with the palace itself, under the ancient rule that a capital must have "the ancestral temple on the left, the altars of soil and grain on the right." ⚠ "左祖右社"规制与1420年建成表述请核实。Emperors came here for every coronation, wedding, and victory to report to the imperial dead. Its three great timber halls — the largest cluster of ancient hall architecture outside the palace itself — still stand in a forest of ancient cypresses, some said to be planted by early Ming hands. In nineteen twenty-four it became the Working People's Cultural Palace ⚠ 年份表述请核实（1924/1950年改名两说，请核实）, but the cypresses don't care about names. Come here when the Forbidden City tickets sell out — same red walls, none of the crowds — and walk the avenue of thousand-year-old-looking trees. ⚠ 古柏树龄"千年"说法夸张，请核实（多称数百年）。

### 41. zhongshan-park — Zhongshan Park & Altar of Land and Grain — landmark: "the five-colored earth altar"
Behind the Forbidden City's other side waits Zhongshan Park — and its heart is the strangest altar in Beijing: a square terrace filled with soil of five colors — red, black, white, green, yellow — one for each direction of the empire plus the center. This was the Altar of Land and Grain, built in fourteen twenty-one, where emperors sacrificed for the realm's harvests; the five-colored earth was brought from every province, making this small platform the empire in miniature. ⚠ 1421年建成与五色土纳贡表述请核实。When the empire fell, the park renamed itself for Sun Yat-sen — Zhongshan — and opened to the public. Today it's Beijing's most civilized park: cypress alleys, peony beds in late April ⚠ 牡丹花期4月下旬表述请核实, pavilion teahouses, and elderly choruses whose repertoire spans revolutionary songs and love ballads. Enter from the Tiananmen side; you'll be inside the walls of imperial Beijing in one minute — and holding the empire's soil in one glance.

### 42. baiyun-guan — White Cloud Temple — landmark: "the temple's triple gateway under old pines"
White Cloud Temple is the Vatican of Chinese Taoism — the headquarters of the Complete Perfection school, and a working monastery since the thirteen hundreds. Its story centers on Qiu Chuji, the Taoist master who crossed deserts and mountains at age seventy-three to counsel Genghis Khan — the khan, impressed, granted him this temple and exemption from taxes ⚠ 丘处机1220-1223年西行与成吉思汗赐居白云观表述请核实. The temple's origins reach back to the Tang dynasty, though fire and rebuilds keep rewriting its fabric. Inside: halls of the Taoist immortals, the grave shrine of Qiu Chuji, and one beloved ritual — hunt the three stone monkeys carved around the courtyard; locals rub them for luck, and finding all three is said to take a lifetime of visits. ⚠ "摸猴"民俗为通行讲法，可保留。At Spring Festival, the temple fair here is Beijing's oldest Taoist celebration. Under the pines, incense smoke and quiet chanting — a completely different Beijing than the palaces next door.

### 43. dongyue-temple — Dongyue Temple — landmark: "the Temple of the God of Mount Tai's entrance screen wall"
Across from the CBD's glass towers stands Dongyue Temple — dedicated to the god of Mount Tai, the emperor of all China's sacred peaks, and judge of souls. Founded in the Yuan dynasty, completed in the thirteen twenties, its fame is a strange and wonderful corridor: seventy-six side halls once held hundreds of clay figures showing what happens after death — the seventy-six departments of the underworld, where every sin had its office and every good deed its record. ⚠ 七十六司与元代始建（1319年动工/1323年落成为通行说法）请核实。When the figures were destroyed in the twentieth century, the temple's endless written pleadings to the god survived — visitors can read real prayers, complaints, and thank-you notes from centuries past. ⚠ 祈愿牌/文书存世表述请核实。Now the Beijing Folk Museum, the temple still bustles with worshippers at New Year, and its stone tablets — more than a hundred — crowd the courtyards like a stone library. Hell has never been so interesting, or so well documented.

### 44. big-bell-temple — Big Bell Temple (Great Bell Museum) — landmark: "the Yongle Great Bell hanging in the bell gallery"
On Beijing's north side hangs the largest bronze bell in China — some say the king of all bells. The Yongle Great Bell, cast around fourteen twenty under the Ming emperor Yongle, weighs some forty-six tonnes, stands nearly six meters tall, and carries a hidden miracle: its inner and outer surfaces are engraved with Buddhist sutras — over two hundred twenty thousand characters, said to be flawless in calligraphy. ⚠ 46.5吨/高5.7米/铭文22.7万字数据通行说法，请核实。Scientists still marvel at how fifteenth-century foundries cast it in one piece. Struck, it rings for minutes, a sound once heard across the old city; today it sounds only on New Year's Eve, one hundred and eight chimes to end the year. ⚠ 跨年敲钟108响传统表述请核实。The temple around it, from the Yongzheng era, now serves as the Ancient Bell Museum — three floors of bells spanning four thousand years of Chinese bronze. Come for the big one; stay to realize bells were an empire's clock, alarm, and anthem at once.

### 45. zhihua-temple — Zhihua Temple — landmark: "the Zhihua Temple's black-glazed roof and main gate"
Zhihua Temple is a Ming dynasty time capsule with a soundtrack. It was built in fourteen forty-four by Wang Zhen, the most powerful court eunuch of his age — ostensibly a family temple, built, some said, in defiance of rules that eunuchs should build none. ⚠ 1444年王振建庙与"宦官不得建庙"背景表述请核实。Its roofs wear black glazed tiles — a color rank reserved for special favor — and its halls survive almost untouched, one of the most complete Ming wooden temple complexes in the city. Its greater treasure is heard, not seen: Zhihua Temple's Music, the Beijing "jing music" performed by monk lineages for over five hundred years, passed mouth-to-ear across generations and now listed as national intangible heritage. Short recitals are still played here on selected days — sit in a fifteenth-century hall and hear Ming-dynasty sound. ⚠ 演出场次不定期（常为工作日固定场次），行前请核实。Hutong-quiet, crowd-free, and unforgettable: this is old Beijing's most scholarly secret.

### 46. fayuan-temple — Fayuan Temple — landmark: "the Fayuan Temple's main gate and bell tower"
Fayuan is the oldest temple in inner Beijing — founded in the Tang dynasty in six forty-five by the emperor Taizong, mourning soldiers lost in a disastrous Korean campaign; it was called the Temple for Comforting the Loyal before gaining its present name, "the Source of Buddhist Law." ⚠ 645年唐太宗敕建悯忠寺表述为通行说法，请核实。Over thirteen centuries, it has been a prison, a poetry salon, a seminary — today it houses the Chinese Buddhist Academy, and young monks still study in its courtyards. The temple is loved in spring, when its famous lilacs bloom and poets have gathered under them for centuries; the writer Li Ao's novel about the temple made it famous far beyond Beijing. ⚠ 李敖《北京法源寺》小说关联表述请核实。Statues range from a giant seated Buddha to bronze pieces centuries apart. Hidden in the hutongs near the Muslim quarter, it sees few tourists — come at reading hour, when chanting rolls softly across courtyards of ancient trees, and thirteen hundred years sit very lightly on one small temple.

### 48. wanshou-temple — Wanshou Temple (Beijing Art Museum) — landmark: "the temple's riverside gate on the Long River"
Wanshou Temple, the "Temple of Longevity," sits on the Long River — the waterway Ming and Qing emperors sailed between the Forbidden City and the Summer Palace. Built in fifteen seventy-seven, it became the imperial rest stop on that voyage: emperors would land here, burn incense, and take tea before continuing upstream to their gardens. ⚠ 1577年万历年间始建与皇家行宫表述请核实。That river link left it serene and graceful — pagoda, halls, gardens, and centuries of imperial gifts. Today it houses the Beijing Art Museum, its exhibits rotating through bronzes, paintings, porcelain, and Buddhist art from the city's collections; the courtyard gardens reward slow walkers year-round, and the old stage once rang with opera for imperial birthdays. ⚠ 长期修缮与重开状态近年有变动，行前请核实开放情况。Its neighborhood, Suzhou Street, ties into the river walk toward the Summer Palace — one quiet hour here, then the water onward: you'd be repeating the emperor's afternoon, at a fraction of the budget.

### 49. tanzhe-temple — Tanzhe Temple — landmark: "the ancient ginkgo known as the Emperor Tree"
The saying goes: "First there was Tanzhe Temple; then came Beijing city." This temple in the Western Hills really is older than the capital — founded in the Jin dynasty, over one thousand seven hundred years ago by tradition ⚠ 建于西晋"1700余年"为景区通行说法，学术上有争议，请核实表述口径 — making it the oldest temple in the Beijing region, when this valley was wilderness and the city was yet to exist. Its courtyards climb a wooded hillside: the Hall of Dragons, a pond fed by the old dragon well, and its legends. The star is the Emperor Tree — a ginkgo about a thousand years old ⚠ 帝王树树龄约千年为通行说法，请核实 — which, tradition says, sprouted a new branch for each new emperor's accession. Empress Dowager Cixi came here; poets came here; now you come here — about an hour from the city, best in autumn when the valley turns gold and the temple smells of pine and incense. The mountain walk beyond is easy and lovely. Beijing's oldest resident has earned your half day.

### 50. jietai-temple — Jietai Temple — landmark: "the great ancient ordination altar"
Jietai, the Ordination Temple, holds one of Chinese Buddhism's most solemn objects: its great ordination altar — a marble platform where, for over a thousand years, monks and nuns took their full vows. Tradition ranks it among the three greatest ordination platforms in China ⚠ "三大戒坛"通行说法请核实，其余两处（泉州开元寺等）表述请核实 — and the temple that holds it, founded in the Tang dynasty in the seven hundreds, was long the licensing authority of the northern Buddhist world: no proper monk north of the Yangtze, the saying went, without Jietai's certificate. ⚠ 此"通行说法"色彩较强，建议软化为"北方佛教受戒中心之一"。The temple sits in hillside quiet west of the city, its courtyards famous for ancient pines with names as good as their shapes — the Free Activity Pine, said to sway when a branch is touched anywhere; the Pine That Embraces the Pagoda, grown around stone. ⚠ 名松典故为景区讲解通行内容，请核实。Come in combination with nearby Tanzhe Temple — two ancient temples, one valley, one unhurried afternoon. The incense here burns for discipline, not wishes; you'll feel the difference.

### 51. dajue-temple — Dajue Temple — landmark: "the temple's clay Buddha hall under the magnolias"
High in the Western Hills sits Dajue, the Temple of Enlightenment — founded in the Liao dynasty over nine hundred and fifty years ago ⚠ 辽咸雍四年1068年建为通行说法，请核实 and loved ever since for its water, its silence, and its flowers. Emperors came here to escape the capital's heat; poets came for the spring. The temple's fame is botanical: its magnolias — planted, tradition says, in the Qing dynasty — bloom each April into the most famous white blossoms in Beijing, drawing crowds for a festival that is essentially perfume and awe. ⚠ 玉兰花期4月与玉兰节表述请核实。Its clear spring still runs through the garden, feeding the ponds where emperors dabbled; the library hall keeps a Qing-dynasty set of scriptures; the main hall keeps serene Liao-and-Ming-era clay Buddhas. ⚠ 无量寿佛殿造像年代表述请核实。Less than an hour from the city, Dajue makes the perfect half-day: temple in the morning, hillside lunch, and Beijing left far below. Spring for the magnolias; autumn for the ginkgo; winter for having the mountain almost to yourself.

### 52. biyun-temple — Temple of Azure Clouds — landmark: "the Diamond Throne Pagoda on the hillside"
Climb past Fragrant Hills' gates and you'll find Biyun Temple — the Azure Cloud Temple — cascading up the mountainside in six rising terraces, each courtyard promising the next one something better. Founded in the Yuan dynasty era ⚠ 始建于元代（元至顺二年1331年为通行说法）请核实, it grew sumptuous under Ming eunuchs and Qing emperors. Two treasures rule. First: the Hall of Five Hundred Arhats — life-sized clay monks, each with a different face and mood, arranged like a wooden crowd; tradition says your fortune matches the arhat you count to from where you enter. ⚠ 数罗汉民俗为通行讲法，可保留。Second: the Diamond Throne Pagoda at the top, an Indian-style stone terrace of the eighteen hundreds with, inside, the memorial to Dr. Sun Yat-sen — his body lay in state here in nineteen twenty-five before burial in Nanjing, and the hall remembers him. ⚠ 1925年停灵与金刚宝座塔1748年建成两事并存，请核实表述。Combine with Fragrant Hills for one full mountain day — temple, forest, summit, and history in one climb.

### 53. wofu-temple — Wofo Temple (Temple of the Reclining Buddha) — landmark: "the great bronze reclining Buddha"
At the foot of the Western Hills, inside Beijing Botanical Garden's grounds, lies Wofo Temple — the Temple of the Reclining Buddha. Its treasure is exactly what the name promises: a bronze Buddha, more than five meters long, lying on one side in the posture of the historical Buddha's final rest — the Nirvana pose — one arm pillowing his head, serenity itself in cast metal. Cast in the Yuan dynasty around thirteen twenty-one ⚠ 1321年至治元年铸造为通行说法，请核实，重量数据（约54吨）请核实，各来源出入大，建议模糊处理, it is among the largest bronze reclining Buddhas in China, and the hall that holds it keeps a dim, hushed atmosphere that fits the subject. The temple began in the Tang dynasty ⚠ 唐代始建（贞观年间说与开元说并存）请核实口径 and its gardens — magnolias in spring, wintersweet blooming in the cold of January — make it a four-seasons detour. Combine with the botanical garden's conservatory next door: garden morning, bronze afternoon, and the quietest famous Buddha in Beijing.

### 54. yunju-temple — Yunju Temple — landmark: "the stone sutra slabs in the sutra vaults"
Deep in Fangshan district, southwest of Beijing, Yunju Temple guards one of the strangest and greatest undertakings in religious history: the stone sutras — Buddhist scriptures carved onto stone slabs, generation after generation, for a thousand years. A monk named Jingwan began the project in the Sui era ⚠ 静琬刻经始于隋末（约605年前后）为通行说法，请核实, fearing the faith's texts would burn as others had; his successors continued until the Ming, carving more than fourteen thousand stone slabs and storing them in mountain vaults and a tower of buried scriptures. ⚠ 石经14278块通行统计数字，请核实。The result: the world's largest library carved in stone, sometimes called "the Dunhuang of Beijing." The temple also keeps relics said to include Buddhist sarira ⚠ 舍利出土与展出状态请核实（1981年发现佛舍利为通行说法）. It's two hours from the city — the farthest spot in this guide — but scripture-readers, print-lovers, and pilgrims of persistence call it Beijing's most moving museum. The stones have outlasted ten dynasties. Your train ride will feel brief.

### 55. lugou-bridge — Marco Polo Bridge & Wanping Fortress — landmark: "the stone lions on Lugou Bridge's balustrades"
The bridge beneath your feet is eight hundred years old — built by the Jin dynasty, completed around eleven ninety-two ⚠ 1192年建成通行说法，请核实 — and famed twice over. First, beauty: Marco Polo described it in wonder, saying no bridge on earth matched it, and so the West named it after him. Its balustrades carry hundreds of stone lions, no two alike — a Chinese tradition says they are too many to count, and visitors have argued the exact number for centuries. ⚡ 狮子数量"501只"为1980年代清点数字，可作趣味表述。Second, history: just beyond its western gate, shots fired on the night of July seventh, nineteen thirty-seven, began the full-scale war between China and Japan — the Marco Polo Bridge Incident, the opening of the Second World War in Asia. The Wanping fortress beside it preserves that night; the Museum of the War of Chinese People's Resistance inside its walls tells the eight years that followed, soberly and powerfully. Walk the bridge at dawn — the lions in morning light, the river valley quiet — and let one small bridge hold both the silk-road wonder and the war that changed the world.

### 56. zhoukoudian — Peking Man Site at Zhoukoudian — landmark: "the excavated cave mouth of Dragon Bone Hill"
Fifty kilometers southwest of the capital, a hill called Dragon Bone Hill changed how humanity sees itself. In the nineteen twenties, excavations here unearthed Peking Man — Homo erectus who lived in these caves hundreds of thousands of years ago, used fire, and left the fossil evidence that made one of the twentieth century's great scientific stories. The first complete skullcap was found in nineteen twenty-nine by Pei Wenzhong, after three days of delicate work in the cave mouth before you. ⚠ 裴文中1929年12月2日发现头盖骨为定论，请核实；北京猿人年代"约70-20万年前"通行说法请核实。Tragically, the original fossils vanished in nineteen forty-one amid wartime chaos — their loss remains one of science's great mysteries ⚠ 化石1941年丢失为定论，表述可保留 — but the site itself, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since nineteen eighty-seven, still yields finds, and the on-site museum rebuilds their world: fire pits, stone tools, and the famous reconstructions. Half a day from the city, best combined with Yunju Temple nearby. Stand at the cave mouth: this is where your morning commute connects to deep time.

### 57. cuandixia — Cuandixia Ancient Village — landmark: "the stone village rising up the hillside, seen from the viewing platform"
Ninety kilometers west of Beijing ⚠ 距市区约90公里通行说法，请核实, a whole Ming-and-Qing village sits intact in a mountain fold: Cuandixia, founded centuries ago by a single family — the Han clan — whose descendants still live in its stone courtyards today. The village's name begins with the rarest character in China: 爨, meaning "cooking stove," twenty-nine strokes of homework nightmares and local pride. Built of mountain stone along a hillside shaped — say the feng shui minded — like a gourd or a tablet, its seventy-some courtyards step up the slope in gray stone and black tile, linked by alleys worn smooth by centuries of feet. ⚠ 明代建村（永乐年间说）与70余座院落为通行说法，请核实。Filmmakers discovered it long ago; slow travelers followed. Come for a day trip — walk the village spine, climb to the viewing platform for the whole panorama, eat a farmhouse lunch — or stay overnight in a courtyard guesthouse and hear actual silence. It is the Beijing countryside's best-preserved memory, hiding three mountain bends from the highway.

### 58. yuandadu-park — Yuan Dadu City Wall Ruins Park — landmark: "the earthen wall remnant among the trees"
This long, green line across the city's north is a ghost: the earthen wall of Kublai Khan's capital. In twelve sixty-seven, the Mongol emperor built Dadu — "the Great Capital" — the planned metropolis that Beijing has been ever since; its walls ran for miles in rammed earth. When later dynasties rebuilt the city smaller, the northern wall was left outside, and centuries of weather softened it into the long hill you can walk today ⚠ 1267年始建大都与北城墙遗存为定论，表述可保留. The park along it stretches for kilometers: cypress walks, bike paths, and a famous spring scene — the Crabapple Blossom Creek, where thousands of crabapple trees flower along a waterway each April and half the city arrives to picnic under them. ⚠ 海棠花溪4月花期与地点（元大都城垣遗址公园内）为通行说法，请核实。Look closely at the grassy mounds: this is the thirteenth century, in earth form. Seven hundred years of history, at jogging height — the most accessible ruin in Beijing, and free.

### 59. huguang-guild-hall — Huguang Guild Hall — landmark: "the opera stage inside Huguang Guild Hall's courtyard"
Off the streets south of Tiananmen hides a perfect wooden opera house from imperial times: Huguang Guild Hall. Guild halls were hometown clubs for merchants and scholars away from home — this one served merchants from Hubei and Hunan, founded in eighteen oh seven ⚠ 1807年嘉庆年间创建为通行说法，请核实 — and its courtyard stage hosted the greatest names of Peking opera's golden age; the acoustics of its all-wood, nail-free structure were part of the show. ⚠ "谭鑫培等曾在此演出"通行说法请核实。Today the hall is the Beijing Opera Museum — costumes, scripts, masks, and the stories of the art's two-century rise — and its stage still performs: on scheduled evenings, real Peking opera plays here to a modest, lucky audience. ⚠ 现今演出场次与票务不定期，行前请核实。For first-timers, this small hall beats the grand theaters: every seat close, every gesture visible, tea served as it was a century ago. Combine with the nearby Liulichang cultural street for one old-Peking evening. The dynasty is gone; the show, happily, is not.

## 城区核心（14 个）

### 1. tiananmen-square — Tiananmen Square — landmark: "the Gate of Heavenly Peace"
You are standing on Tiananmen Square — one of the largest public squares on Earth, big enough to hold nearly half a million people. For centuries this was the front courtyard of the imperial city, closed to ordinary people. Today it belongs to everyone. To the north rises the Gate of Heavenly Peace, from which Mao Zedong proclaimed the founding of the People's Republic of China in 1949 — his portrait still hangs there today. Around you: the Great Hall of the People on the west, the National Museum of China on the east, the Monument to the People's Heroes at the center, and the Mausoleum of Mao Zedong to the south. Every sunrise, crowds gather for the flag-raising ceremony, timed to the moment the sun breaks the horizon. ⚠ 如广场临时管制，解说词会提到"开放时段以现场公告为准"——请确认是否保留此句。Walk north toward the gate, and behind it waits the Forbidden City — the next chapter of your walk.

### 2. forbidden-city — The Forbidden City (Palace Museum) — landmark: "the Hall of Supreme Harmony"
You've just stepped into the Forbidden City — the largest palace complex on Earth, and for nearly five hundred years, a place no ordinary person was allowed to enter. Built by the Yongle Emperor of the Ming dynasty and completed in 1420, it was home to twenty-four emperors of the Ming and Qing dynasties. Its old name says it all: the Purple Forbidden City — forbidden because entering without permission meant death. Behind its walls: nearly nine thousand rooms of halls, gardens, and courtyards, aligned on a single north-south axis that runs through the heart of Beijing. Since 1987 it has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the Palace Museum inside holds over a million treasures. Walk up the central axis: the Hall of Supreme Harmony — the throne hall where emperors held court — is the largest wooden hall in China. Give it at least two hours. You'll only see a fraction.

### 3. jingshan-park — Jingshan Park — landmark: "the Wanchun Pavilion at the hilltop"
You're entering Jingshan Park — and it holds the single best view in Beijing. This hill right in front of you is man-made: five peaks of soil dug out to create the moats of the Forbidden City directly to the south. Climb to the pavilion on top, turn around, and you'll see it — the entire Forbidden City, its golden roofs stretching to the horizon, laid out like a stage set. It also holds a tragic story. In 1644, as rebels stormed the city, the last Ming emperor, Chongzhen, fled into this park and hanged himself from a tree — and with him, the Ming dynasty ended. The tree standing today is a descendant of the original, marked with a plaque. Come at sunset if you can: first the panorama, then the drums of the city at dusk. Jingshan is where Beijing reveals its perfect geometry.

### 4. beihai-park — Beihai Park — landmark: "the White Dagoba on Jade Flower Island"
Welcome to Beihai Park — one of the oldest imperial gardens in China, over a thousand years in the making. Right in front of you, across the water, rises its icon: the White Dagoba, a Tibetan-style stupa built in 1651 by the first Qing emperor to honor a visit by the Dalai Lama. ⚠ 建造缘由（崇德/顺治时期建以迎五世达赖）请核实表述。The lake itself — Beihai means "Northern Sea" — was the playground of emperors for centuries: they boated in summer and skated across the ice in winter. This garden perfected a Chinese art: recreating an entire imaginary world in miniature — islands as immortal mountains, water as the sea. Look for the Nine-Dragon Wall near the north shore, one of the finest glazed-brick walls in China, its nine dragons each a different color. locals come here at dawn to dance, sing opera, and practice calligraphy with water brushes on the stone paths. Join the morning; it's magical.

### 5. shichahai — Shichahai Lakes — landmark: "the Yinding Bridge, the Silver Ingot Bridge"
You've arrived at Shichahai — three linked lakes that have been the soul of old Beijing for seven hundred years. The name means "ten temple lake", for the ten Buddhist and Taoist temples that once lined these shores. This is where the Grand Canal once ended: in the Yuan dynasty, barges unloaded silk and grain right here, making this the busiest harbor of the empire's capital. Today the lakes are Beijing's favorite escape — willow-lined shores, hutong lanes, and in winter, locals skating across the frozen water on traditional ice sledges. Cross the little arched Yinding Bridge — the "Silver Ingot Bridge" — one of the most photographed corners of the city, where old men fish in the morning and young people gather at dusk. All around you, the hutong neighborhoods that grew up around the lakes are among the best preserved in Beijing. Slow down here. This is the Beijing people fall in love with.

### 6. nanluoguxiang — Nanluoguxiang — landmark: "the south entrance gate of Nanluoguxiang"
This is Nanluoguxiang — an eight-hundred-year-old hutong from the Yuan dynasty, and one of the oldest alleys still standing in Beijing. Its name means "South Luogu Alley", and its layout is remarkable: the main alley runs about eight hundred meters north to south, with sixteen side hutongs branching out like the legs of a centipede — locals have called it "the Centipede Street" for centuries. This chessboard plan dates to the founding of Yuan-dynasty Dadu, the city that became modern Beijing. What makes it special is the mix: gray-brick courtyard homes — siheyuan — standing beside cafés, bakeries, and design shops. Each side hutong hides its own stories: former residences of generals, painters, and playwrights are marked with plaques as you wander. ⚠ 具体名人故居清单请实施阶段核实。Go at a quiet hour if you can, and step into the side alleys — that's where the eight hundred years actually live.

### 7. yonghe-temple — Yonghe Temple (Lama Temple) — landmark: "the Hall of Harmony and Peace"
You've reached the Yonghe Temple — Beijing's most vibrant Tibetan Buddhist temple, still thick with incense smoke and prayer. Its story begins as a royal residence: built in 1694 for the prince who would become the Yongzheng Emperor. When he took the throne, the palace could no longer be a home — so in 1744 it was converted into a monastery of the Gelug school, the "Yellow Hat" tradition of Tibetan Buddhism. That's why it looks like a palace: golden roofs and red walls, Han Chinese and Tibetan styles fused into one. Inside, don't miss the Guinness-record giant: an eighteen-meter statue of the Maitreya Buddha, carved from a single sandalwood trunk — it took three years to transport the log to Beijing. ⚠ 高度与"26米/18米地上"表述不一，实施阶段以馆方数据核实。Come early, breathe the sandalwood air, and spin a prayer wheel as you go — locals will show you how.

### 8. guozijian-confucius-temple — Imperial College & Confucius Temple — landmark: "the Biyong Hall"
You're walking along Chengxian Street — Beijing's only surviving ancient board-and-plate paved lane — toward two treasures sharing one wall: the Confucius Temple and the Imperial College, the Guozijian. On your way in, pass under the Archway of Superior Learning — one of the only wooden archways in Beijing not painted in imperial yellow. The Confucius Temple, founded in 1302, honors China's great teacher: look for the nearly two hundred stone steles engraved with the names of more than fifty thousand scholars who passed the imperial examinations over five centuries — the largest honor roll you'll ever see. Beside it, the Imperial College was the empire's highest school, where the sons of nobility studied for those very exams. At its center stands the Biyong, a square hall on an island of water, where the emperor himself lectured — the "hall of learning surrounded by a moat". Ancient cypresses, some over seven hundred years old, shade it all.

### 9. temple-of-heaven — Temple of Heaven — landmark: "the Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests"
You've just stepped into the Temple of Heaven — and for five hundred years, this is where China's emperors came to speak with the sky itself. Twice a year, the Ming and Qing emperors left the Forbidden City in solemn procession and traveled here to pray for good harvests — for the empire, this was the most important ritual of the year. At the heart of the complex rises the Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests: a circular masterpiece of wood, famous for being built without a single nail, standing on three tiers of marble. The circle stood for heaven; the square grounds below, for earth. When UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site in 1998, it called the temple "a masterpiece of architecture and landscape design" that shaped the Far East for centuries. But here's the secret locals know: this is also a park. On weekend mornings, thousands gather — dancing, singing opera, playing chess under ancient cypresses. Stay a while. Heaven can wait.

### 10. qianmen-dashilan — Qianmen & Dashilan — landmark: "the Zhengyangmen Archery Tower"
You've arrived at Qianmen — "the Front Gate" — the grand southern gateway of old Beijing, and just beyond it, Dashilan, the city's oldest shopping street, six hundred years in the making. When the Qing court banned night life inside the imperial city, merchants, theaters, and teahouses all crowded here instead — and Dashilan became the entertainment capital of old Peking: the cradle of Peking opera, home to the city's first cinemas. The old brands are still trading: Tongrentang, the pharmacy of imperial physicians since the seventeen century; Zhangyiyuan tea; Neiliansheng, the cloth-shoe maker whose slippers once suited Qing officials. ⚠ 老字号创立年代（同仁堂1669/内联升1853/张一元1900）请实施阶段核实后定稿。Look down as you walk — the street's name comes from the big wooden gates that once closed it at night. And look up at the Zhengyangmen archery tower behind you: part of the medieval city wall that once ringed all of Beijing. Duck into the side alleys — the old theater courtyards still echo.

### 11. summer-palace — The Summer Palace — landmark: "the Tower of Buddhist Incense on Longevity Hill"
Welcome to the Summer Palace — the greatest imperial garden that survives on Earth, and Beijing's masterpiece of water and hills. Covering nearly three square kilometers — three-quarters of it water — this was the summer retreat of the Qing court, who fled the heat of the Forbidden City for the breezes of Kunming Lake. Everything here is deliberate: the lake was expanded by hand, and the earth dug from it was piled into Longevity Hill before you. Walk the Long Corridor — seven hundred meters of painted walkway, its beams holding thousands of classical scenes — said to be the longest painted corridor in the world. ⚠ 长廊728米/14000余幅彩画请核实。At its end, the Marble Boat waits: a stone vessel where the Empress Dowager Cixi famously lounged — paid for, critics said, with money meant for the imperial navy. And the story ended as critics warned. Come at golden hour, rent a boat, and see what emperors saw.

### 12. old-summer-palace — Old Summer Palace (Yuanmingyuan) — landmark: "the Great Fountain ruins, Dashuifa"
You're entering the Old Summer Palace — Yuanmingyuan, the "Garden of Gardens" — and its most beautiful ruins. In the eighteen century this was the largest imperial garden ever built: hundreds of halls, pavilions, and libraries spread across a thousand acres of hills and lakes, housing centuries of art and treasure. Emperor Qianlong even commissioned a section of European palaces — baroque fountains and marble facades designed by Jesuit court artists — the buildings whose stones you see before you. Then, in October 1860, Anglo-French forces looted and burned the palace during the Second Opium War — three days of fire that erased the glory and left the marble skeleton you walk among today. China keeps these ruins unrestored, on purpose: they are remembered as a monument to that era, and schoolchildren still come here to learn why. Walk slowly. This place is not about what stands — it's about what was lost, and what a garden once meant.

### 13. 798-art-zone — 798 Art Zone — landmark: "the main factory gallery with Bauhaus sawtooth roofs"
Welcome to 798 — where a cold-war electronics factory became China's contemporary art capital. These vast halls were built in the nineteen fifties with East German assistance, in genuine Bauhaus style: look up at the sawtooth roofs, angled north so uniform, soft light would pour into the workshops — a factory designed like a photographer's studio. The plant made military electronics for decades, then fell silent. In the early two-thousands, starving artists discovered what collectors now pay fortunes for: those chimneys, pipes, and red propaganda slogans painted on factory walls became the raw aesthetic of Chinese contemporary art. Galleries moved in, then museums, then the world — today 798 hosts hundreds of galleries and design shops, and its exhibitions range from ink painting to digital art. No ticket needed to wander. Grab a coffee in a former workshop, and let the factory walls surprise you — this is where today's China argues with its own history, in paint and steel.

### 14. wangfujing — Wangfujing — landmark: "the Wangfujing pedestrian street clock tower"
You've stepped onto Wangfujing — Beijing's most famous shopping street, seven hundred years of commerce in motion. Its name means "Wang Family Well" — for the ten wells that once served this district when it was home to princes and their mansions in the Ming and Qing dynasties. When modern China opened up, this became the first great shopping boulevard of Beijing — and today it's a mile of department stores, century-old food brands, and neon, entirely pedestrianized in its southern stretch. This is the place to taste Beijing fast: the food halls nearby serve everything from roast duck to candied hawthorn skewers — tanghulu — and the brave queue for the snack street's more adventurous offerings. ⚠ 王府井小吃街近年改造升级，营业状态请核实。For a quieter contrast, slip into St. Joseph's Church at the street's east side — a baroque-style church from sixteen fifty-five, still holding mass amid the shopping crowds. Seven centuries in one street — go hungry, and go slow.

## 城区延伸 + 郊区（11 个）

### 15. badaling-great-wall — Badaling Great Wall — landmark: "the highest watchtower of the Badaling section"
This is it — the Great Wall of China, at Badaling, its most famous gateway. The wall beneath your hands is not one wall but many, built and rebuilt across two thousand years; the brick wall you're standing on dates to the Ming dynasty, the fifteen to sixteen hundreds, when Beijing was the capital it guarded. The old saying goes: "He who has never been to the Great Wall is not a true man" — 不到长城非好汉 — and Badaling is where emperors, presidents, and millions of travelers have earned that title. Watch how it behaves like a living creature: it rides the spine of the mountains, its towers placed within sight of each other — smoke signals once leapt from tower to tower, carrying news of invasion to the capital at astonishing speed. Badaling was the first section opened to visitors, in the nineteen fifties, and it's fully restored and ramped for every age. Climb to the highest tower you can — the wall disappears into the mountains both ways. That view is the China postcard, and you're standing in it.

### 16. mutianyu-great-wall — Mutianyu Great Wall — landmark: "the watchtower cluster at Mutianyu ridge"
You've chosen the connoisseur's Great Wall: Mutianyu. Same Ming-dynasty wall, same mountain-spine engineering — but wrapped in forest instead of the city crowds. This section guarded the northeast approach to Beijing for centuries, and its builders showed personality here: it boasts more watchtowers than most sections, including a rare pair of towers built as one triple-height giant, and the crenellations come in both rectangular and sawtooth patterns — a detail found almost nowhere else. ⚠ 双塔/锯齿垛口细节请实施阶段核实。For centuries this wall slept under vines and pines; restoration in the nineteen eighties reopened it, and today a cable car lifts you up, and — if your inner child insists — a toboggan slide brings you down. Walk east toward tower twenty-three ⚠ 编号请核实 and you'll feel what most visitors never do: silence, wind, and the wall stretching on beyond sight. Come in October, and the mountains burn red with maple and smoke-tree leaves. This is the Great Wall at its most beautiful.

### 17. ming-tombs — Ming Tombs (Sacred Way) — landmark: "the Sacred Way stone animal statues"
You've arrived at the Ming Tombs — the valley where thirteen emperors of the Ming dynasty sleep beneath the mountains. The site was chosen by feng shui masters: mountains arc around it like dragon wings, and a river winds through — an emperor's paradise. Every visitor walks the Sacred Way first: a several-kilometer avenue lined with colossal stone statues — two rows of creatures, from camels and elephants to mythical qilin and xiezhi, each carved from single blocks five centuries ago. They stand guard in pairs: one kneeling, one standing — so that even a tired spirit guard never sleeps on duty. ⚠ "一站一跪轮值"传说为导游常用讲法，请核实可否保留。At the end, the archway and the dragon-phoenix gates frame your approach to the tomb valleys. Only one tomb has ever been opened: Dingling, the Underground Palace, excavated in the nineteen fifties — step down into the vaulted chamber of stone and stand where the Wanli Emperor was laid to rest with his two empresses. ⚠ Dingling 开放状态请核实（近年或有调整）。The mountains hold the rest — thirteen emperors, one valley, five hundred years of silence.

### 18. prince-gong-mansion — Prince Gong Mansion — landmark: "the opera house courtyard of Prince Gong Mansion"
You've entered Prince Gong's Mansion — the most complete princely residence surviving in Beijing, and the most dramatic biography of any house in the city. It began as the estate of Heshen, the most corrupt — and richest — official of the Qing empire: his private fortune, the story goes, exceeded the imperial treasury itself. ⚠ "和珅家产超国库"为传统说法，请保留为传说表述。When the emperor's son took the throne, Heshen was forced to take his own life, and the mansion passed to Prince Gong — its name ever since. Walk the front halls, then slip through to the surprise behind: a vast private garden of rockeries, ponds, and pavilions — including a Western-style gate, rare for its time — and a hidden stone that legend ties to imperial misfortune. ⚠ 花园西洋门/福字碑细节请核实。Don't miss the courtyard opera house — all-wood acoustics, no nails in its hollow frames — where performances still play today. One mansion, two dynasties, one spectacular downfall. Beijing history, condensed into a single walled garden.

### 19. drum-bell-towers — Drum Tower & Bell Tower — landmark: "the Drum Tower's great timekeeping drums"
These two towering gray buildings — the Drum Tower and the Bell Tower — were once the clock of Beijing. For centuries, city life moved to their sound: at dusk the great drums beat, at dawn the great bell rang — twenty-five booming strikes marking the opening and closing of the city gates. Inside the Drum Tower you can still see the original timekeeping instruments: the water clock that measured the hours for centuries, and ranks of great drums — the largest, a single hide over two meters across. ⚠ 水铜刻漏/更鼓25面、主鼓直径表述请核实。The towers date to the Yuan dynasty — the older you look, the more impressive that is — and both were rebuilt under Ming and Qing emperors. Climb the steep stairs — they were built for duty, not comfort — and you'll stand where the night watch once stood, holding the whole city in one glance: hutongs breaking like waves around lakes to the south. In this neighborhood, time still moves slowly. These towers are the reason.

### 20. houhai — Houhai — landmark: "the lakeside bar street of Houhai's south shore"
This is Houhai — "the Back Sea" — where old Beijing comes out to play at night. One of the Shichahai lakes, Houhai spent centuries as an aristocratic playground: princes built their gardens here, opera barges floated on summer nights. Today the tradition continues in modern costume: the south and east shores glow with lantern-lit bars and live music venues, while just one hutong back, families play chess under the willows as if the neon didn't exist. That contrast is the magic of Houhai — and your evening plan writes itself. At dusk, walk the shore from the Yinding Bridge: swans and pedal boats at golden hour, then music rising as the lights come on. In winter the lake freezes solid and becomes Beijing's great ice playground — sledges, skates, and sixty-year-old hockey games. ⚠ 冰场开放期大致为12月底-2月，视天气而定，请核实表述。Come for a drink, stay for the people-watching — Houhai is where Beijing's daytime shyness ends.

### 21. liulichang — Liulichang Cultural Street — landmark: "the archway at the east entrance of Liulichang"
You've slipped into Liulichang — the quietest famous street in Beijing, and its most literate. For five hundred years this has been the haunt of scholars: in Qing dynasty times, examination candidates flooded the city and lodged nearby, and booksellers, calligraphers, and antique dealers set up shop here to feed the hungry minds. The name means "Colored Glaze Factory", from the Ming dynasty kilns that once fired imperial roof tiles on this very ground. Today it's a street of art: brushes ground by hand, ink stones, rubbings, seals carved while you wait, and silk-mounted paintings. Its crown jewel is Rongbaozhai — the "Studio of Glorious Treasures" — a three-hundred-year-old workshop where artists still produce woodblock prints using techniques passed down for generations; each print may take months and dozens of carvings. ⚠ 木版水印工艺描述请核实。Come early, watch a seal-cutter work, and leave with a brush made the old way. This is Beijing's slow, scholarly soul — hiding in plain sight.

### 22. panjiayuan-market — Panjiayuan Antique Market — landmark: "the main gate arch of Panjiayuan Market"
Welcome to Panjiayuan — Beijing's greatest treasure hunt, and the largest antique and crafts market of its kind in China. What began as a spontaneous "dirt market" — vendors spreading their wares on the ground before dawn — is now a city block of thousands of stalls: Mao-era memorabilia, porcelain, jade, seals, faded family photos, Buddhist statues, lamps, lacquer, and things no category can hold. The unwritten rule: much of what you'll see is old-styled rather than old — so browse for beauty, bargain with humor, and never buy what you can't afford to love as a replica. The market's soul is the weekend: vendors arrive before sunrise, and serious hunters come with flashlights. ⚠ 开市时间（平日/周末及黎明开市惯例）请核实。Prices start high and drop with a smile and a walk-away — halving the first quote is normal. Bring cash-sized patience and small bills, keep your valuables front, and give yourself two hours. Everything here has a story — and half the fun is the vendor telling it.

### 23. ancient-observatory — Beijing Ancient Observatory — landmark: "the bronze instruments atop the observatory platform"
You've found one of the most unlikely survivals in Beijing: a working astronomical observatory from the Ming dynasty, standing beside the second ring road since fourteen forty-two. For nearly five hundred years, astronomers climbed this platform nightly to track the heavens for the emperor — because in imperial China, the sky was politics: eclipses and comets were omens, and the calendar was the throne's business. The bronze instruments on the roof are the stars of the show — armillary spheres, sextants, and a celestial globe, cast in the late sixteen hundreds under the direction of Ferdinand Verbiest, a Flemish Jesuit priest who served the Kangxi Emperor as court astronomer. ⚠ 仪器为1673年南怀仁监制6件+后续增制，表述请核实。Look closely: European engineering wrapped in Chinese dragons and clouds — East and West collaborating three centuries before "globalization". Few visitors find this place; those who do, climb a medieval tower and touch the instruments that once mapped the sky for an empire. Astronomy, diplomacy, and survival — all in one rooftop.

### 24. olympic-green — Olympic Green (Bird's Nest & Water Cube) — landmark: "the Bird's Nest National Stadium"
Welcome to Olympic Green — Beijing's monument to its two Olympic Games, two thousand eight and two thousand twenty-two — the only city on Earth to host both the Summer and Winter editions. Before you rises the Bird's Nest — the National Stadium — its tangled steel lattice designed by architects Herzog & de Meuron with artist Ai Weiwei as consultant. ⚠ Ai Weiwei 参与角色为"艺术顾问"，后与其产生分歧；表述请核实。It cost years of engineering to make randomness look effortless: every beam is placed, none is decor. Beside it, the glowing blue cube is the Water Cube — the National Aquatics Center, where swimming world records famously fell like dominoes in two thousand eight — and which was later transformed, astonishingly, into the "Ice Cube" curling venue for the Winter Games. ⚠ 水立方→冰立方改造为冰壶场地表述请核实。At night the whole park lights up — the nest glows red, the cube shimmers blue, and families fly kites on the great axis between them. Come after sunset: this is Beijing's modern skyline of dreams, and it photographs best in the dark.

### 25. fragrant-hills — Fragrant Hills Park (Xiangshan) — landmark: "the Incense Burner Peak temple ruins at the summit"
You've arrived at Fragrant Hills — the Beijing autumn that everyone talks about. This imperial park at the foot of the Western Mountains has been a retreat since the Jin dynasty, eight hundred years ago — emperors built temples here, and the Kangxi and Qianlong emperors made it a summer escape of palaces and pines. The park's name comes from the hill's twin peaks — said to smell of sweet incense, though what draws the crowds is color: every late October into November, the mountainsides of smoke trees turn crimson, and half of Beijing hikes up to walk inside that red. ⚠ 红叶最佳期（10月中-11月上旬）与黄栌smoke tree表述请核实。The climb to Incense Burner Peak — five hundred fifty meters, stairs most of the way — rewards you with the whole Beijing plain at your feet. On the way, visit the Temple of Azure Clouds at the park's edge, where the body of Dr. Sun Yat-sen, father of modern China, lay in state in nineteen twenty-five. ⚠ 孙中山碧云寺停灵1925年表述请核实。Arrive early on autumn weekends — the red leaves deserve your sunrise, not your traffic jam.
